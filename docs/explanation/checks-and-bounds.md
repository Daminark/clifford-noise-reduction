# Checks and bounds

CliNR implements each chunk $C_k$ of the circuit by gate teleportation.
It prepares the *resource state* $(I \otimes C_k)|\Phi^+\rangle^{\otimes n}$ apart from the data, measures `r` of its stabilizers, the checks, and teleports the data through it only if every check passes.
This page explains why a check catches a fault, what the two families of checks guarantee, and how those guarantees combine into the bounds that {func}`clinr.bounds.plog_bound` and {func}`clinr.bounds.overhead_bound` compute.
We follow the paper's Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 (Delfosse and Tham, [arXiv:2407.06583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06583)) and say where our bounds part from them.

## What a check detects

The resource state has $2n$ qubits, and its stabilizer group $S$ has $2^{2n}$ elements, generated by the propagated Bell stabilizers $X_i \otimes C_k X_i C_k^\dagger$ and $Z_i \otimes C_k Z_i C_k^\dagger$.
A fault leaves some Pauli error $E$ on the resource.
Measuring a check $g \in S$ flips its outcome exactly when $E$ anticommutes with $g$.

For a fixed $E$, the map that sends $g$ to $0$ if $g$ commutes with $E$ and to $1$ otherwise is a homomorphism from $S$ to $\mathbb{F}_2$.
If $E$ commutes with all of $S$, then $E$ lies in $S$ up to a phase, because $S$ is a maximal commuting group, and $E$ leaves the resource unchanged.
Otherwise $E$ damages the resource, the homomorphism is onto, and its kernel has index 2: **exactly half of $S$ anticommutes with $E$.**
No fault damages the state while commuting with every stabilizer.

## The two families of checks

{func}`clinr.checks.draw_checks` draws the `r` checks of a chunk independently, with replacement, from one of two families.

**Uniform over the group.**
A check is a uniformly random element of $S$, the identity included, as in the paper.
Exactly half of $S$ commutes with a damaging $E$, so one draw misses with probability $q = 1/2$.
The price is weight: an element of $S$ acts on up to $2n$ qubits, and a weight-$w$ check costs $w$ two-qubit gates.

**Restricted.**
The family of the paper's Sec. V ({func}`clinr.checks.restricted_family`) keeps, for each Bell pair $i$, the triple of propagated $XX$, $ZZ$ and their product, the propagated $YY$: $3n$ elements, each of weight 1 on the bare half and at most $n$ on the other, so of weight at most $n + 1$.
Commutation with $E$ is a homomorphism, so on a triple it takes the values $(a, b, a \oplus b)$, which contain either no 1 or exactly two.
The $2n$ pair stabilizers generate $S$, so a damaging $E$ anticommutes with at least one of them, and its triple then contains exactly two anticommuting elements.
One draw from the $3n$ therefore misses with probability at most

$$q = 1 - \frac{2}{3n},$$

and enumerating every Pauli on $2n$ qubits shows the bound is attained at $n = 1, 2, 3$.
At $n = 1$ the family is the whole group without the identity, with a miss of $1/3$; beyond, the restricted miss approaches 1 while the uniform one stays at $1/2$.
The paper runs its numerics with this family, while Theorem 1 covers uniform checks only.

## The bound on the logical error rate

Split the operations of a chunk into three regions: the *resource* (preparing the Bell pairs and applying $C_k$), the *checks*, and the *teleportation* that moves the data through the resource after the last check.
The resource and the checks together form the *protected region*, of $m$ operations.
For a region with $n_1$ single-qubit gates, $n_2$ two-qubit gates, $n_m$ measurements and $n_p$ preparations, the probability that some operation in it faults is

$$g = 1 - (1 - p_1)^{n_1} (1 - p_2)^{n_2} (1 - p_m)^{n_m} (1 - p_{\mathrm{prep}})^{n_p}.$$

An accepted shot of the chunk ends in a logical error only if a resource fault escapes all `r` checks, a check fault escapes the later checks, or the teleportation faults.
A resource fault leaves an error fixed before the checks are measured, which all `r` draws miss with probability at most $q^r$.
A fault in the $j$-th check gadget leaves an error that depends on that gadget's check, but the later checks are drawn independently of it, so they all miss it with probability at most $q^{r-j}$; summing over the gadgets gives the escape factor

$$e_r = \sum_{j=1}^{r} q^{r-j} = \frac{1 - q^r}{1 - q}.$$

The shot is accepted at least whenever no operation in the protected region faults.
So, conditioned on acceptance,

$$p_k \le \frac{g_{\mathrm{resource}} \, q^r + g_{\mathrm{check}} \, e_r + g_{\mathrm{teleport}}}{1 - g_{\mathrm{protected}}},$$

with $g_{\mathrm{check}}$ the fault probability of one check gadget at the family's worst-case weight.
{func}`clinr.bounds.plog_bound` returns the sum of $p_k$ over the chunks: a logical error in the output needs one in some chunk.
At $t = 1$ this is the paper's Lemma 1, Eq. (B1); {mod}`clinr.accounting` counts each region's operations from the circuit as built.

Without checks every attempt is accepted, so there is nothing to condition on.
At $r = 0$ the bound is instead the probability that some operation in the resource or the teleportation faults, which is below the expression above at $r = 0$.

## The bound on the gate overhead

The protocol measures all `r` checks of an attempt before deciding (see {doc}`../adr/0001-run-every-check-before-deciding`), so every attempt at chunk $k$ costs its $m_k$ operations, and the number of attempts is geometric with mean $1/a_k$, where $a_k$ is the acceptance probability.
The teleportation, $4n$ operations, follows the last check and is paid once.
By Wald's identity the expected number of operations per gate is

$$\omega = \frac{1}{s} \sum_{k} \left(4n + \frac{m_k}{a_k}\right),$$

and {func}`clinr.bounds.overhead_bound` replaces each $a_k$ by its lower bound $1 - g_{\mathrm{protected}}$, with every check at worst-case weight.
{func}`clinr.estimators.gate_overhead` evaluates the same expression with the acceptance and check weights measured.
Without checks $a_k = 1$, and the bound is exact.

## How these bounds differ from Theorem 1

Theorem 1 states the two bounds for one error rate $p$ and $t$ chunks of $s_0 = \lceil s/t \rceil$ gates each.
Ours differ from them as follows, and {doc}`differences` collects these with the package's other departures from the paper:

- **Four rates**, one per kind of operation, where the paper uses one.
- **Exact chunk sizes**: each chunk is charged its own gates, where the paper charges every chunk $s_0$.
- **$4n$ for the teleportation**, where the paper's $5n$ includes $n$ Pauli gates applying the correction; we track the correction as a Pauli frame instead ({doc}`../adr/0002-deliver-the-output-up-to-a-pauli-frame`).
- **The preparation convention**: by default $|+\rangle$ is a reset and a Hadamard, two operations where the paper counts one; with `native_plus=True` the counts match the paper's.
- **No conditioning without checks**: at $r = 0$ every attempt is accepted, so our bound on the logical error rate does not divide by a probability of acceptance and our overhead bound is exact, where Theorem 1 keeps the factor $(1 - p)^{-m_0}$ in both.
- **The restricted family is bounded too**, with the per-draw miss above, where Theorem 1 covers uniform checks only.

Under the paper's own conventions (one rate, `native_plus=True`, uniform checks), our bounds never exceed Theorem 1's, which the test suite checks over random circuits and parameters.

Theorem 1 takes at most $s$ chunks, since any beyond the $s$-th would be empty.
This implementation allows more ({doc}`../adr/0003-allow-more-chunks-than-gates`), and our overhead bound charges each empty chunk its teleportation.
